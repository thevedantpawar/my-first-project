The leak

Most med spas don’t have a lead problem. They have a response problem.

The inquiries are already coming in. Here is where they quietly walk back out — usually into the clinic three blocks away that answered first.

The slow reply

An inquiry lands at 9 PM. Someone gets to it at 11 the next morning. By then she has messaged two other clinics, and one of them answered while she was still holding her phone. Interest has a shelf life, and it is measured in minutes.

The missed call

The phone rings while your injector is mid-treatment and the front desk is checking someone out. No voicemail, no text back, no record it ever happened. That number is not in a list anywhere. It is just gone.

The one-and-done follow-up

She asked about pricing, said she wanted to think about it, and nothing ever went out again. Not because your team does not care — because nobody has time for a fourth touch. Ready-to-book patients go cold in the gap.

None of this is a staffing failure. It is a coverage gap — and it runs every evening, every weekend, and every hour your team is with a client.

How it works

One lead’s journey, start to finish.

The same five steps run on every inquiry, at 2 PM on a Tuesday and at 11 PM on a Sunday.

  1. 01

    Inquiry arrives

    Website form, Instagram DM, Meta lead ad, or a call nobody could pick up. Every source lands in one place.

  2. 02

    Instant personalized reply

    A reply goes out in under 60 seconds by text or DM — her name, her treatment, her question. Not a form response.

  3. 03

    Qualification

    It asks the questions you approve: treatment interest, timing, budget range, how she found you. You set the list.

  4. 04

    Consultation booked

    She picks a slot from your live availability. It lands on your calendar directly. No back-and-forth, no double bookings.

  5. 05

    Follow-up until booked

    If she does not book, the sequence keeps going for 14 days — then stops. It also stops the moment she opts out.

Anything clinical goes straight to your staff. The system only uses answers you approve in writing.

Boundaries

Built to stay in its lane.

A system talking to your patients needs hard limits. These are written into the build, not left to judgment.

Contact and scheduling information only
The system works with names, phone numbers, email addresses, what she asked about, and when she is booked. It does not touch medical records, charts, photos, or treatment history. Those stay where they are, in the systems your clinic already uses for them.
No clinical advice. Ever.
Questions about conditions, medications, contraindications, suitability, or expected results are never answered by the system. They are handed to your staff with the full conversation attached, so a person picks up exactly where she left off.
Your calendar, your tools, your control
Everything is built on top of the booking software, phone number, and calendar you already run. Your calendar stays the source of truth. Access is limited to what the system needs to do its job, and you can revoke it at any time.

Questions

The ones clinic owners actually ask.

Will AI say something wrong to a patient?

It can only send what you approve. The system works from a written FAQ you sign off on, line by line. Anything outside that list — conditions, medications, contraindications, expected results, pricing you have not approved — is not answered at all. It is handed to your staff with the full conversation attached. The failure mode is “a human replies,” not “the system guesses.”

Does this replace my front desk?

No. It covers the moments they cannot: the 9 PM DM, the call that came in during a treatment, the fourth follow-up nobody has time for. Every conversation that needs a person still goes to a person. Your team keeps the relationship and walks in to a calendar that is already filling.

What software do you work with?

Whatever you already run. The system is built around your booking software, calendar, and phone number rather than replacing them — common setups include Boulevard, Vagaro, Zenoti, Mindbody, Square, Google Calendar, your website forms, and Instagram or Meta lead ads. On the audit call I check your exact stack before anything starts, and I will tell you plainly if something will not connect cleanly.

How long until it’s live?

14 days. Days 1–3 map your inquiry sources and draft the replies, questions, and approved FAQ. Days 4–10 build it and connect it to your calendar and phone number. Days 11–14 test against real inquiries and get your sign-off. You review and approve every message before a single one reaches a lead.

Is patient data safe?

The system holds contact and scheduling information only: name, phone, email, what she asked about, and when she is booked. No charts, no records, no clinical notes — that content is never connected to it. Access is limited to the accounts that need it, kept inside the tools your clinic already controls, and revocable by you at any time.
