# The 3-week sprint — first client

You want a client in 21 days. Cold email cannot do that, and I'd rather say so than let the
warm-up quietly eat your three weeks.

**This does not replace the Snov.io campaign.** `micronsai.com` warms in the background
exactly as planned. This runs on top, on channels that need no warm-up at all.

---

## 1 · Why cold email can't hit 3 weeks

Two reasons, and the second is the real one.

**Warm-up.** 3–4 weeks before the first send. Day 21 is roughly the day you'd start.

**The funnel, even after that.** At benchmark rates, 739 leads across the full 17-week
campaign produce roughly 30–55 replies, 15–25 real conversations, and 1–3 clients. Week 1 is
125 emails. That's about 6 replies, maybe 3 conversations. Statistically it is not where your
first client comes from — the *follow-ups* carry more than half the replies, and those land
in weeks 3 through 6 of sending.

Cold email is a 3-month machine. It is the right machine and it is nearly built. It is not a
3-week machine and no amount of pushing makes it one.

---

## 2 · What can actually work in 21 days

Three channels, none of which need a warmed domain. You already have everything for all three.

### Phone — the one that matters

**736 of the 739 leads have a phone number in the workbook.** Zero warm-up, zero
deliverability, zero domain reputation. A med spa front desk picks up. That is the whole
argument.

Thirty dials a day for fifteen working days is 450 calls into a list you have already
researched — you know each clinic's closed days, review count and rating before you dial.
Almost nobody calling them has that.

### Email from your existing inbox — hand-sent, not automated

Here's the thing worth understanding: **warm-up exists because of volume and automation, not
because of email.** Fifteen individual emails a day, typed and sent from an inbox you've used
for a year, is not a deliverability event. It's just correspondence.

You can start Monday. The copy is written — 739 hand-written emails sitting in the CSV. Send
them one at a time from your normal address. Fifteen a day, no tool, no tracking pixel.

**Do not automate this and do not exceed ~15/day.** The moment it looks like a campaign it
becomes one.

### Instagram DM

Med spas live on Instagram, and the owner usually runs the account personally — so a DM often
reaches the decision-maker directly, which the phone and the `info@` inbox do not. Slower to
scale, no warm-up needed, and it's where this audience actually is.

## 3 · The sprint

**60 leads. Phone first, email as the follow-through, IG as the third touch.**

`sprint_call_list.csv` — the top 60 by rank, wrong-fit excluded, all with phones. Each row
carries the clinic's timezone, the calling window in UTC, the hook, and the opening line
lifted from that lead's own hand-written email. Blank columns at the end for logging.

Timezone spread: 35 Eastern, 10 Central, 7 Mountain, 8 Pacific. Calling 9–5 local across the
US means roughly **14:00–00:00 UTC**. Check that against your own hours before you commit to
30 dials a day — if that window is the middle of your night, this plan needs reshaping and
you should tell me now rather than in week 2.

### Daily rhythm

| | |
|---|---|
| 30 dials | ~2.5 hours |
| 15 hand-sent emails | ~45 min, to that day's no-answers |
| 5 Instagram DMs | ~20 min |
| Log every outcome | in the sprint CSV, same day |

Roughly four hours a day. Three weeks of that is the sprint.

### The maths that follows

30 dials/day × 15 days = 450 calls. Med spa reception connect rates run 25–35%, so
~110–150 real conversations. Of those, maybe 15–25 get you past the front desk to an owner
or manager. Of those, 5–10 book a call. Of those, **1–2 could sign inside 21 days** — if
your pricing exists and the offer is easy to say yes to.

That is a real path to one client. It is not a certainty, and anyone who tells you it is, is
selling something.

## 4 · The call

You do not have names — the workbook has none. Ask by role.

> "Hi — is the practice manager around? … I'll be quick, I'm not selling software over the
> phone. I noticed **[their specific thing]**. Is that costing you bookings, or have you got
> it covered?"

`sprint_call_list.csv` gives you **[their specific thing]** per lead. Examples from the top
of the list:

- **Elase, Salt Lake City** — "you've got 38 locations and Sugar House still shows Sunday dark"
- **Refresh, Jupiter** — "you're closed both weekend days across three sites"
- **Suddenly Slimmer, Phoenix** — "you've been open since 1988, so you've got the biggest
  dormant patient list in Phoenix and probably nothing running against it"
- **Kumi, Katy** — "your BBB profile sits at C+, and it's for not answering a complaint
  rather than the complaint itself"

Then shut up and let them answer. If it's not costing them, say thanks and hang up — that's a
real answer and you've saved you both time.

**When they ask what it is:** one sentence. *"An agent that answers your phone after hours,
quotes your actual prices, and books into your calendar as pending so your team still
confirms."* Not five automations. One thing.

**"Send me an email."** That's a win, not a brush-off. It converts a cold email into a
replied-to one. Send it that hour, from your own inbox, referencing the call in the first
line. Those are the 15 daily emails.

**"Who else uses this?"** Same answer as the playbook: you'd be early, there's no case study,
you're not going to invent one, and you'll show them the build instead. Offer the
early-customer discount and ask for a reference in exchange.

## 5 · Pricing is now blocker number one

It was third on the list. At a 3-week deadline it's first.

You cannot close a client in 21 days without a number. Not a range you improvise on the call
— a decided price you can say out loud on day one without hesitating. The same goes for
whether you sign a BAA; that question arrives on the first serious call, not the tenth.

**Decide both today.** Everything else in this sprint is execution; these two are the only
things that can make the whole three weeks unclosable.

---

## 6 · What day 21 should actually look like

Set the target where it's honest:

| Outcome | Likelihood |
|---|---|
| 450 calls made, list worked, learning banked | certain, if you do the work |
| 5–10 discovery calls booked | likely |
| 1–2 verbal pilot agreements | plausible |
| 1 signed, paying client | possible, not likely |

The most valuable thing this sprint produces might not be the client. It's hearing the same
objection fifteen times in week one and rewriting the offer around it — which then makes all
739 emails land better when the machine does start.

## 7 · Meanwhile, in the background

None of this delays the campaign:

1. Buy `micronsai.com`, point it somewhere real, create the five mailboxes
2. Start warm-up — it runs untouched for the full 3–4 weeks while you're on the phone
3. Verify the 739 addresses through Snov.io's verifier during week 2 or 3
4. Register Google Postmaster Tools
5. Build the 8 campaigns in week 3, ready to launch the day warm-up finishes

Day 22, the sprint has taught you what actually lands, and the machine starts with better
copy than it would have had.
