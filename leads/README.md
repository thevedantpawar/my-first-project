---

## Read this first — the strategic call that shapes this list

The obvious move is to target "funded AI startups." **That is the worst segment for you.** A company that raised $130M to build AI agents has ten engineers who do exactly what you do, in-house, for free. You will lose on price and on credibility.

The money is in the opposite profile: **well-funded, ops-heavy companies whose product is *not* AI.** Mortgage brokerages, clearing firms, multi-site clinics, rental platforms, logistics networks, manufacturers. They have:

- Real revenue and real budget
- Enormous manual back-office load (docs, KYC, onboarding, reconciliation, scheduling)
- Engineering teams fully consumed by the core product
- A recent raise with a mandate to scale *without* linear headcount growth

That is the ICP this list is built against. A handful of AI-native companies are included, but flagged and scored lower, with a different angle (overflow/integration capacity, not core build).

# BONUS 1 — The 3 niche segments where you'll close fastest

### 🥇 1. UAE PropTech & mortgage/rental fintech

2. **They're all funded within 18 months** and all publicly committed to scaling transaction volume.

4. **Lower competitive saturation.** Dubai has nothing like the density of automation consultancies that London and NYC do. You'll be one of few credible options rather than one of two hundred.

### 🥈 2. Multi-site physical expansion (US)
**Who:** Blank Street (100+ stores → California), Small Door Veterinary (6 new clinics), Senra Systems (third factory)

Everyone chases software companies. Meanwhile, funded physical-footprint businesses have huge budgets, genuinely manual operations, and **almost no internal automation capability** — so you are not competing with an in-house team.

Site-opening playbooks, staff onboarding, per-site demand forecasting and inventory are all well-understood automation problems with directly measurable ROI (waste percentage, time-to-full-utilisation).

**Do this:** Track funding announcements that mention new locations, factories or clinics. That phrase is your buying signal. It appears in almost every growth-round press release and almost nobody is selling against it.

# BONUS 2 — 3 outreach strategies tailored to these leads

### Strategy 1 — The "Use-of-Funds" play *(highest conversion; start here)*

Almost every company on this list **published what they intend to do with the money.** That is stated, dated, on-the-record buying intent, and virtually nobody uses it.

Flow48 said the round funds *"alternative data sources and advanced risk assessment tools."* Keyper said it funds *"adoption among institutional landlords and large residential portfolios."* RQD said expansion into *"North America, Asia and the Middle East"* plus tokenisation. 01Health hired a Head of US Commercial Operations.

**Execution:**
1. Open by quoting their stated plan back to them, verbatim, in one line.
2. Name the specific operational bottleneck that plan will hit — be concrete, not generic.
3. Offer the fix in one sentence.
4. Close with a question about *their* metric, not a request for a meeting.

**Why it works:** It proves you did work before contacting them, it's impossible to mistake for a template, and asking for a number ("what's your current deals-per-broker?") gets replies where "do you have 15 minutes?" does not. Ending on a genuine question is the single highest-leverage change you can make to cold outreach.

### Strategy 2 — The "Don't Compete With Your Engineers" reframe *(for AI-native targets)*

For FYLD, Origin, Mal, Charta and Auger, the default pitch actively backfires — you are telling engineers you can do their job better than they can. Invert it completely.

**The frame:** *"You have world-class AI capability. What you don't have is the headcount to do customer-side integration while shipping roadmap. I do the integration work so your engineers stay on product."*

This converts you from threat to relief. It's also the honest description of what these companies actually need, which is why it lands. Position around **deployment engineering, connector build-out and customer-side integration** — never around AI capability.

**Practical tip:** target the COO, VP Customer Success or VP Delivery here, not the CTO. The CTO's incentive is to defend in-house scope; the delivery leader's incentive is to unblock their queue. Same company, opposite reaction to the same email.

### Strategy 3 — The Dubai proximity + ecosystem-referral loop

Dubai's funded-startup ecosystem is small, interconnected and heavily in-person. It rewards presence in a way that London and New York do not.

**Execution:**
1. **Be physically present.** DIFC FinTech Hive, Hub71 events, Dubai FinTech Summit and GITEX. In this ecosystem a coffee outperforms fifty emails, and you can meet several targets from this list in a single week.
2. **Work the shared cap tables.** Property Finder and Ellington Properties both appear in Keyper's and Stake's investor sets. MEVP appears in both. Investors introduce portfolio companies to vendors who have already delivered for another portfolio company — that's a free, warm, high-trust channel.
3. **Publish one credible regional artefact.** A short, specific teardown — *"What happens to a UAE mortgage application between submission and approval, and which seven steps are automatable"* — does more in this market than any volume of outreach. It gets forwarded internally, which is how you get inbound from people you never contacted.
4. **Sequence deliberately:** land one UAE PropTech client → deliver something demonstrable → ask directly for the two named introductions → repeat.

**Why this beats the same effort spent on US/UK outreach:** you are competing against far fewer credible automation providers, and relationship-led buying is the regional norm rather than the exception. Your effort-to-outcome ratio is simply better there.
