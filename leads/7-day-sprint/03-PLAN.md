# TRACK A — where the $1,500 most likely actually comes from

**Probability if you work it properly: 50–65%.**

I'd be doing you a disservice if I let you bet rent money on cold-emailing Series B founders across a weekend. The reliable sources of $1,500 in four working days are:

### A1. Your warm network — do this within the next hour
Every past client, every colleague, every founder you've helped, every WhatsApp group you're in. One message:

> "Quick one — I've got capacity for one automation project starting Monday. Fixed fee, five days: I map a manual workflow, cost it out, and build one working piece. $1,500. If anyone in your network is drowning in manual ops right now, send them my way?"

**One warm introduction outperforms a hundred cold emails on a four-day clock.** This is the single highest-value thing you will do today.

### A2. Fast-deciding small buyers
You excluded these from the brief, and you were right — for building a *pipeline*. For *cash this week*, they are the realistic source. A local agency, clinic, recruitment firm or e-commerce operator will pay $1,500 for a workflow fix on a Tuesday, because the owner decides alone and pays by card immediately.

Sources that convert inside a week: local business owners' WhatsApp/Slack groups, r/smallbusiness and r/automation, Upwork or Contra automation gigs (search "n8n", "Zapier", "Make.com", "workflow automation"), and LinkedIn posts from people complaining about manual work.

### A3. Post publicly today
One LinkedIn post, written as a useful observation rather than an advert:

> "Spent the week researching 20 funded companies across the US, UK and UAE for a piece on where automation budget actually sits.
>
> The pattern that surprised me: the companies that need workflow automation most aren't the AI startups. They're the funded, ops-heavy businesses whose product isn't AI — mortgage brokerages, clearing firms, multi-site clinics, rental platforms.
>
> They have real revenue, enormous manual back-office load, and engineering teams entirely consumed by the core product.
>
> The AI startups build it themselves. The proptech company processing $7bn a year across two regulatory regimes doesn't — and the cost of not doing it shows up in deals-per-broker.
>
> Happy to share the research if it's useful to anyone."

This does three jobs at once: it generates inbound, it's genuine credibility, and it warms the exact cold leads you emailed this morning — several of them will see it.

# IF FRIDAY ARRIVES WITH NO REPLIES

Don't panic and don't discount. Do this instead:

1. **Check deliverability.** If you sent from Gmail to 20 founders in one morning, some went to spam. Check whether your domain has SPF/DKIM set up.
2. **Shift weight to Track A.** Cold outreach has a floor on how fast it can work, and you've hit it.
3. **Post the research publicly** (the LinkedIn post above, or a short teardown of one workflow). Inbound from content is genuinely faster than cold email in a four-day window.
4. **Lower the ask, not the price.** Instead of the $1,500 teardown, offer a free 20-minute workflow review — and convert on the call. The price never moves; the commitment does.
