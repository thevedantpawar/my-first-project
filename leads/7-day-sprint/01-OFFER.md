# The Offer — built to close in days, not quarters

## The problem with your current offer

A $5,000–$50,000 automation project triggers procurement, security review, a budget line and usually a second stakeholder. That is a 4–12 week path. It is the right offer for the pipeline. It cannot produce $1,500 this week.

So you need a second offer that sits **below the friction threshold** — small enough for one person to approve on a card, valuable enough that they say yes on the call.

---

# THE 5-DAY AUTOMATION TEARDOWN — $1,500

> **What it is:** I take your single most expensive manual workflow, map it end to end, put a number on what it costs you per month, and hand back a ranked automation plan — plus one working prototype of the smallest piece, so you can see it run rather than take my word for it.
>
> **Time:** 5 working days. One 45-minute kickoff call, one 30-minute handover call. Nothing else needed from your team.
>
> **Price:** $1,500, fixed. Paid upfront by card.
>
> **The credit:** 100% credited against any build you commission within 60 days. If you go ahead, the teardown was free.

### What you get on day 5

1. **The process map** — every step, handoff and wait state in the workflow, as it actually runs, not as the SOP claims
2. **The cost number** — hours per month, converted to fully-loaded salary cost. One number you can put in a board update.
3. **The ranked plan** — every automatable step, scored by hours saved against build effort, with a build estimate against each
4. **A working prototype** — the smallest high-value piece, built and running on your real data (or a realistic sample), yours to keep whether or not you hire me again

### What it costs you if you don't

Nothing. That's the point — the 60-day credit means the only way to lose money is to do the teardown and then ignore what it says.

## Why each design choice is there

| Choice | Why |
|---|---|
| **$1,500, not $5,000** | Sits under almost every discretionary approval limit. A founder or Head of Ops expenses it without asking anyone. This is the single biggest lever on speed. |
| **Paid, not free** | Free audits get accepted and then never scheduled — no skin in the game, no calendar priority. A paid engagement gets a kickoff call booked this week. |
| **Fixed price** | No scoping call, no proposal, no negotiation. Removes the three things that add two weeks. |
| **5 days, not 2 weeks** | Short enough to start Monday and finish Friday. Fits inside the buyer's current sprint, so it doesn't need planning. |
| **Working prototype included** | This is the whole strategy. A document gets filed. Something that *runs* gets shown to the CTO — and that's how the $15k build gets commissioned. |
| **60-day credit** | Removes the "is this worth it if we just hire you anyway" objection completely. It converts the teardown from a cost into a free trial with a deadline. |
| **Card upfront** | The difference between *signed* in 7 days and *paid* in 7 days. Never invoice for this. |

## Handling the four objections you'll actually get

**"Send me a proposal."**
> "Happy to — though the whole thing is one page and I can talk you through it now in two minutes. The scope is fixed and the price is fixed, so there's not much a document adds. Want me to just walk you through it?"

*This is the most important one to handle live. "I'll send a proposal" is how a 7-day deal becomes a 30-day deal.*

**"We'd need to loop in [person]."**
> "Makes sense. Worth knowing it's $1,500 and five days, so it's usually below the threshold where that's needed — but if it helps, I can jump on 15 minutes with them this week."

**"Can you do it cheaper / can we start with a free chat?"**
> "The call's free, always. The teardown is priced where it is because you get a working prototype at the end, not a document — and it's fully credited if you go ahead with a build. So if you're going to work with me at all, it costs nothing."

*Do not discount. A discount at $1,500 permanently anchors your price with a buyer who can pay $50,000.*

**"What if we don't like the findings?"**
> "Then you've got a process map and a costed workflow for $1,500, which is cheaper than any consultancy will do it, and you owe me nothing further. I'd rather find out in five days than five weeks."

## Pricing note — read this before you get tempted

You are selling a $1,500 teardown to companies that just raised $11M to $116M. **The price is not the obstacle.** Speed, trust and risk are.

So never negotiate the $1,500 down. Every discount signals the number was arbitrary, and these are the exact buyers you want anchored high for the six-figure work later. If someone genuinely can't do $1,500, they are not a real prospect for a $15,000 build either — thank them and move on.
